Abstract

Modern economic analyses often require linking centuries-old U.S. tariff schedules — written in archaic prose and coded under multiple, now-obsolete systems — to today's eight-digit Harmonized System (HS). I present the first fully automated concordance that threads every tariff line from the 1789 Tariff Act through four major coding overhauls into the 2023 HS vocabulary. The pipeline "walks" backwards one calendar year at a time: each description is (i) modernized with a GPT-4 prompt, (ii) embedded with the sentence-transformer MPNet, and (iii) matched to its nearest neighbor in the previous year using a composite similarity score. When that score dips below a specified threshold, I first apply a hierarchical HS fallback — matching at the 2-, 4-, or 6-digit level appropriate to the historical regime — before any remaining low-confidence records trigger a Selenium-based query to the U.S. Census HS-Classifier, with GPT selecting among the most probable codes the website outputs. On three public schedules — 1789 (free text), 1963 (TSUS-8), and 1989 (HS-8) — the method attains 65%, 75%, and 90% similarity scores respectively. The resulting open-source concordance and Python package let researchers map any historical description to a ranked list of 2023 HS codes with confidence scores, enabling consistent long-run trade analyses without manual recoding.

Key results

Across the 1989–2022 window where modern HS codes are directly comparable, my pipeline retains the original HS-6 code in roughly 79% of cases. The remaining 21% concentrate in years immediately following major schedule rewrites: in 1995 and 1996, between 23.5% and 28.2% of mappings fall below my composite-similarity threshold of 0.5, reflecting the textual reorganizations the HS underwent that decade.

The pipeline is built around four cooperating components: a Selenium scraper that pulls historical schedules from the U.S. International Trade Commission and Census archives; a GPT-4 prompt that modernizes archaic phrasing one year at a time so the back-walk never has to bridge more than ~12 months of linguistic drift; an MPNet sentence-transformer that produces embeddings for every description; and a composite similarity score that combines cosine similarity with hierarchical HS-digit retention. The whole chain is deterministic given fixed temperature and a frozen embedding model.

For low-confidence matches, the system falls through to a hierarchical HS backoff — matching at the 6-, 4-, or 2-digit level depending on which regime the source year predates — and only escalates to live Selenium queries against the Census HS-Classifier when none of those layers pass the similarity threshold. Empirically, this two-stage fallback resolves the majority of edge cases without any human in the loop.

The deliverable is an open-source concordance plus a Python package that exposes the full mapping behind a single function call: feed in any tariff description from any year between 1789 and 2023, get back a ranked list of 2023 HS codes with confidence scores. The intended downstream use is long-run trade-policy work that would otherwise require months of hand-coded historical alignment.
